\section{Safety and Regulatory Compliance}

Deploying collaborative robotics in an industrial setting requires strict adherence to international safety standards, primarily \textbf{ISO 10218-1/2} (Safety requirements for industrial robots) and \textbf{ISO/TS 15066} (Collaborative robots).

\subsection{Robot-Robot vs. Human-Robot Collaboration}
It is critical to distinguish that the proposed architecture is primarily a \textit{Robot-Robot Collaboration} (RRC) system. The collaboration occurs between the AR4 and ABB manipulators, while the human operator is physically removed to an IoT control room. 

Because the ABB IRB120 is a standard industrial robot (not inherently a force-limited cobot), the cell cannot rely on Power and Force Limiting (PFL) safety paradigms. Instead, the cell must employ Speed and Separation Monitoring (SSM) and strict safety zones to ensure safe parallel execution.

\subsection{Evaluating the Software Zone Manager}
The implemented \texttt{ZoneDetectionManager} successfully creates logical "Safe," "Approach," and "Handover" zones to prevent robot-robot collisions, triggering MTC resolution when necessary. However, from a regulatory standpoint, standard ROS 2 nodes running on a general-purpose operating system (like Linux) are not generally certified to Performance Level \textit{d} (PL d) or Safety Integrity Level 2 (SIL 2), which are required by ISO standards for human safety.

Therefore, while the algorithmic Zone Manager is highly effective for equipment preservation and operational efficiency, a fully compliant commercial deployment requires hardware-in-the-loop safety integration. This limitation will be addressed in the Future Work section, detailing how the software state machine must be backed by physical safety relays (such as safety laser scanners or light curtains) to override motor power instantaneously in the event of an unpredictable human intrusion.
