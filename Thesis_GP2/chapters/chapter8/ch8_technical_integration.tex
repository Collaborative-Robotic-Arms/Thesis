\section{Technical Integration and Software Commercialization}

Transitioning the academic prototype to a 24/7 factory environment introduces rigorous constraints regarding deterministic cycle times and zero-downtime reliability. While the ROS 2 and MoveIt 2 stack successfully handles the high-level perception and parallel execution, industrial deployment requires a hybrid control architecture.

\subsection{The Hybrid Control Architecture}
In a commercial setting, ROS 2 and MoveIt 2 do not directly drive motor currents or act as the primary safety watchdogs. Instead, the architecture is bifurcated \cite{mdpi2026interoperability}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High-Level Orchestration (The Brain):} Running on an Industrial PC (IPC), ROS 2 manages the YOLOv8 vision processing, MoveIt Task Constructor (MTC) path generation, Digital Twin synchronization, and IIoT cloud communication. The DDS middleware inherent to ROS 2 provides the Quality of Service (QoS) necessary for robust packet delivery in noisy environments \cite{profanter2019protocols}.
    \item \textbf{Low-Level Determinism (The Muscle):} The Cartesian trajectories generated by MTC are transmitted to proprietary OEM controllers (e.g., ABB OmniCore) and safety PLCs. These hardware controllers execute motion with microsecond real-time determinism and enforce hard safety stops independently of the ROS 2 network \cite{mdpi2026interoperability}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Digital Twin Integration Hurdles}
Implementing a real-time Digital Twin requires high-quality data integration and robust IoT infrastructure. Firms must bridge the gap between legacy manufacturing execution systems (MES) and modern telemetry \cite{xu2023survey}. The successful deployment of this cell relies heavily on minimizing latency between the physical encoders and the RViz virtual sandbox, ensuring that pre-execution validation remains perfectly synchronized with the factory floor \cite{researchgate2026digitaltwin}.
