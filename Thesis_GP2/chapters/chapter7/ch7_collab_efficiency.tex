This chapter presents a comprehensive evaluation of the Digital-Twin-enabled dual-arm collaborative assembly system. The performance of the system is quantified through rigorous testing in both the simulated environment and on the physical hardware deployment comprising the ABB IRB120 and AR4 manipulators. The analysis is divided into operational metrics, focusing on the efficiency of parallel execution and part handovers, and safety metrics, validating the robustness of the dynamic collision avoidance architecture.

\section{Collaboration Efficiency}

To quantify the benefits of transitioning from a sequential, single-arm architecture to a multithreaded, dual-arm collaborative cell, several Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) were established. The primary metrics evaluated include total cycle time, parallel execution efficiency, and the reliability of the handover state machine. 

\subsection{Execution Timing and Throughput}

Throughput and cycle time were measured by executing a standardized assembly sequence consisting of [INSERT NUMBER] bricks, distributed between the AR4 and ABB reachable workspaces. The system was benchmarked under two operational paradigms: strictly sequential execution (where only one arm moves at any given time) and the implemented parallel execution framework.

The performance metrics collected include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Total Assembly Time ($T_{total}$):} The absolute time from the initialization of the first pick command to the completion of the final place command.
    \item \textbf{Parallel Utilization Rate ($U_p$):} The percentage of the total assembly time where both manipulators were in simultaneous motion.
    \item \textbf{Arm Starvation/Idle Time ($T_{idle}$):} The cumulative time an arm spent waiting for the other to clear a shared zone or complete a handover.
\end{itemize}
